## Michael

Marketing. Finishing an MBA. Former battle rapper.

I build Claude Skills out of mechanics I learned in rooms with no HR department. Battle rap, DJing, producing, sampling. Technique the culture spent fifty years refining, because the feedback was instant and the cost of being wrong was getting laughed at.

Most people write the essay about it. I shipped the tools.

Seven of them. Public, MIT licensed, installable in one line. They work as a set, in this order.

**[battle-tested](https://github.com/MichaelDoesMarketing/battle-tested)** writes the line.
Marketing copy built with battle rap mechanics. Angle, scheme, setup and punch, rebuttals, and a crowd test that kills anything you can't back.

**[versus](https://github.com/MichaelDoesMarketing/versus)** picks the fight.
Which competitor to take on, whether naming them helps or hurts, and when to stand down. Four verdicts, four kill rules.

**[subtext](https://github.com/MichaelDoesMarketing/subtext)** reads what came back.
What a message is actually saying, with every finding tied to a quoted feature and a mandatory innocent reading before anything ships.

**[sequencing](https://github.com/MichaelDoesMarketing/sequencing)** fixes the order.
DJ set construction for decks, agendas, talks, and pages. Every item gets a job. The walkout mode finds the exact seam where you lose people.

**[sampled](https://github.com/MichaelDoesMarketing/sampled)** checks whose it was.
Whether the idea you're about to publish already belongs to somebody. Sources, dates, and an honest coverage statement every time. It will never tell you something is original.

**[headroom](https://github.com/MichaelDoesMarketing/headroom)** leaves room.
The beat that sounds best alone is the worst beat to rap on. Finds where your deck or template is standing on the toes of the person who has to use it.

**[freestyle](https://github.com/MichaelDoesMarketing/freestyle)** asks if you made it for this room.
Change the name, the company, the occasion. Whatever survives all five swaps was never about them.

Every README has a real demo in it, run on real work, including mine getting taken apart.

Start with battle-tested. It's where the whole thing came from.
